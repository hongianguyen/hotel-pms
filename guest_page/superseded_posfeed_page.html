Kosher, halal & special dietsPlease read

We are not a kosher kitchen and hold no certification — there is no supervision, no separate utensils, and no kosher slaughter available in this province. What we can do honestly: cook without pork or shellfish, keep meat and dairy off the same plate, and prepare any dish vegetarian. Tell reception the evening before so the kitchen can plan around the morning market. Note that fish sauce is used throughout this cuisine, including in dishes with no fish in the name.

Vegetarian, allergies, children

Full vegetarian and vegan versions of every menu. Tell us about allergies when you book a table — fish sauce and peanuts are used widely in this kitchen. High chairs and half portions for children, no charge.

Who was Y Lak

In the M'nông telling, the God of Water lost his fight with the God of Fire and the land went dry. Y Lak — born of a god and a M'nông girl — left his village to find water and atone for his parents. Exhausted, he freed an eel trapped in a rock, and the eel led him to an immense lake full of fish and green things. The M'nông settled around it. The lake carries his name, and so does our kitchen.

Play

Lake & villages

Almost everything here is free and needs nothing but showing up. Reception arranges the rest — morning activities close the evening before.

Running & jogging05:00 & 17:00

A 3 km loop around the camp on dirt, gravel and asphalt, past lotus ponds, paddy fields and coffee gardens. 45–60 minutes, easy going. Sunrise 05:00–06:00 or sunset 17:00–18:00. Meet at Reception or Y LAK. Free, self-guided.

SUP & kayaking05:30

On the calm corner of the lake between the camp and "Crocodile" hill. From 05:30, about an hour — sunrise, M'nông fishermen at work, and water lilies in full bloom from June to November. Free and self-guided; reserve at reception on busy days. Life jackets are compulsory.

Jungle Discovery Trail09:00

A 2 km trail through the forested garden behind the tents and over the hilltop. From 09:00, about an hour, with a guide. Along the way: coucal habitat, bamboo stands, junglefowl, Hopea odorata forest, the lake viewpoint, the farm, and the coffee and cacao plantation. It ends with a herbal tea you pick and brew yourself. Free.

Coffee experience15:30

In the camp's own coffee and cacao garden. From 15:30, 30 to 60 minutes: roast, pound and brew coffee the M'nông way, and walk the garden in harvest season. Included in some packages, otherwise 50,000₫ per person. Please book at reception in advance.

Gong performance18:00 · Fri & Sat

In the Central Garden or at Y LAK House, from 18:00 for about an hour. Songs, dances, M'nông welcome ceremonies and rượu cần tasting. Free every Friday and Saturday; arranged on request for private groups.

Elephants — how we do itNo riding

We don't sell or book elephant rides, and we ask our guests not to buy them elsewhere around the lake. What reception can arrange instead is a visit to a free-roaming herd — you walk with the mahout and watch from a distance, on the elephants' terms.

Villages, boat trips & more

Guided visits to the M'nông villages of Nau and M'Liêng, sunset boat cruises, photography outings and other excursions are arranged at reception, and are included in several stay packages. Please ask before photographing people in the villages — these are homes, not exhibits.

Sleep

Your tent

Canvas walls mean you'll hear the lake, the frogs and the first birds. Don't expect five-star luxury; expect thousand-star nights.

Where you're staying

25 units across 4.8 hectares: 15 Lakeview Tented Lodges, 5 Kpan tiny houses on the lake, 4 Lakefront Bungalows, and Y Liêng House, an 8-berth dormitory by the water. Tents have an en-suite bathroom, a private lake-view balcony, air conditioning and a fan. Bungalows add a living room and a bathtub.

Hot water & power

Hot water is heated by solar power, so it is strongest in the late afternoon and evening. This is an eco-lodge, not a luxury hotel: expect the lake breeze to do some of the work of the air conditioning, and crickets and roosters to serve as the night entertainment and the wake-up call.

Insects, geckos and snakes

This is open nature. You may meet insects, mosquitoes, geckos and, occasionally, snakes. Keep your tent or bungalow properly closed, and carry the flashlight from your room when you walk at night. The geckos are harmless and eat your mosquitoes.

In your room, and at reception

An umbrella, a flashlight and a hat are provided in every room — please use them with care and leave them behind. Jewellery and other valuables can be deposited in the safe at reception, against a receipt. Smoking is not permitted inside the rooms; please use your balcony or front patio. For anything else — laundry, an early breakfast, a late departure — ask at reception.

Children & safety on the water

The camp suits children over 6 best, though younger ones are welcome under their parents' care. Under 6 sharing a bed is free; 6 to 11 is charged half. Life jackets are compulsory for everyone boarding a boat, kayak or paddleboard.

Tread lightly

Our way

LAK Tented Camp was conceived around two goals: preserving cultural integrity, and protecting this environment. The lake is somebody's home before it is anybody's view — we're guests here too.

The bottle in your tent is made to be refilled

Purified water from a natural well, served in refillable bottles. Refill rather than buy — plastic bottles are exactly what this lake does not need.

Minimum build, maximum trees

Only 25 units across 4.8 hectares, built to sit within the local architecture and ecology rather than on top of it, with an ongoing commitment to reforestation. Electricity use is kept deliberately low.

Where your food comes from

The camp grows its own vegetables and fruit, alongside the coffee and cacao garden, and most of it goes straight to the kitchen. You are welcome to pick your own vegetables and cook them. The menu is built on locally sourced ingredients — we won't offer you a thousand international dishes, but what we do serve is fresh.

Who works here

LAK Tented Camp was made for and by local people — the M'nông and neighbouring villagers. Our staff and managers speak Vietnamese, English and M'nông, and some speak French. If the service is sometimes short of polish, they would rather hear your feedback than not. Teach them some English and they will teach you some M'nông.

Three small things that help
  • Avoid single-use plastic — bags, bottles and harsh chemicals all end up in the lake basin
  • Switch off the aircon when you go out; canvas cools down fast
  • Don't feed the wildlife, and keep the noise down after dark — sound travels a long way over water
